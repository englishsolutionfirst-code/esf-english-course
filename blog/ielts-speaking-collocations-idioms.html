IELTS SPEAKING VOCABULARY

IELTS Speaking Band 7+: Powerful Collocations & Idiomatic Expressions

Learn how to use natural collocations and idiomatic expressions to make your IELTS Speaking answers more precise, fluent and natural.

← Back to IELTS Learning Blog

Achieving a Band 7 or higher in IELTS Speaking is not simply about knowing a large number of difficult words. What matters is your ability to use vocabulary naturally, accurately and flexibly when expressing your ideas.

Key idea: A strong IELTS Speaking candidate does not try to use as many difficult words as possible. Instead, they choose natural combinations of words and use them appropriately in context.

Why Are Collocations Important in IELTS Speaking?

A collocation is a natural combination of words that commonly appear together in English. Native speakers tend to use these combinations automatically.

For example, we normally say "make a decision", not "do a decision". We say "heavy traffic", not "strong traffic".

Using appropriate collocations can make your English sound more natural and can help demonstrate a wider range of vocabulary.

50 Useful IELTS Speaking Collocations

1. Pursue higher education

Meaning: to continue studying at university or another advanced institution.

"I'd like to pursue higher education abroad because it would give me access to better academic opportunities."

2. Academic performance

Meaning: how well a student performs academically.

"My academic performance improved significantly after I developed a more consistent study routine."

3. Career prospects

Meaning: opportunities for future career development.

"One reason I chose this field is that it offers excellent career prospects."

4. Gain valuable experience

Meaning: obtain useful knowledge or skills through experience.

"Part-time jobs allow young people to gain valuable experience before entering the workforce."

5. Achieve a work-life balance

Meaning: maintain a healthy balance between professional and personal life.

6. Make a living

25 Natural Idiomatic Expressions for IELTS Speaking

Idioms can make your speaking sound more natural when they are used appropriately. However, the goal is not to force an idiom into every answer.

1. A breath of fresh air

Meaning: something pleasantly different or refreshing.

"Spending a weekend in the countryside is a breath of fresh air after a busy week."

2. Once in a blue moon

Meaning: very rarely.

"I eat fast food once in a blue moon because I prefer home-cooked meals."

3. Get the hang of something

Meaning: learn how to do something.

"It took me a while to get the hang of speaking English in front of an audience."

4. Go the extra mile

Meaning: make an additional effort.

"Good teachers often go the extra mile to help their students succeed."

5. A double-edged sword

Meaning: something with both advantages and disadvantages.

"Social media is a double-edged sword because it connects people but can also be distracting."

6. Break the ice

Meaning: make people feel more comfortable in a new situation.

"A simple joke can help break the ice when you meet someone for the first time."

7. Hit the nail on the head

Meaning: describe something exactly or identify the main point.

"I think you hit the nail on the head when you said that education should be more practical."

8. See eye to eye

Meaning: agree with someone.

"My parents and I don't always see eye to eye on career choices."

9. Cost an arm and a leg

Meaning: be extremely expensive.

"Studying abroad can cost an arm and a leg, especially in major cities."

10. Under the weather

Meaning: feeling slightly ill.

"I was feeling under the weather yesterday, so I stayed at home."

11. On the same page

Meaning: have the same understanding or opinion.

"Before starting the project, we need to make sure everyone is on the same page."

12. A piece of cake

Meaning: something very easy.

"Once you understand the basic rules, the exercise is a piece of cake."

13. In the same boat

Meaning: be in the same difficult situation.

"Most students are in the same boat when they start learning a new language."

14. Go hand in hand

Meaning: two things are closely connected.

"Education and economic development often go hand in hand."

15. Learn something the hard way

Meaning: learn through a difficult or unpleasant experience.

"I learned the hard way that leaving assignments until the last minute is a bad idea."

16. Keep an eye on

Meaning: watch or monitor something carefully.

"I always keep an eye on my spending when I'm travelling."

17. Have a soft spot for

Meaning: have a particular affection for something or someone.

"I have a soft spot for old-fashioned cafés because they have a unique atmosphere."

18. Not my cup of tea

Meaning: not something you particularly enjoy.

"I'm afraid extreme sports aren't really my cup of tea."

19. A change of pace

Meaning: something different from your normal routine.

"Going hiking at the weekend gives me a nice change of pace."

20. Out of the blue

Meaning: unexpectedly.

"An old school friend contacted me out of the blue last month."

21. Put yourself in someone's shoes

Meaning: imagine what another person is experiencing.

"We should put ourselves in other people's shoes before judging them."

22. Take something for granted

Meaning: fail to appreciate something because you assume it will always be available.

"People sometimes take clean water for granted until they experience a shortage."

23. A win-win situation

Meaning: a situation in which everyone benefits.

"Working from home can be a win-win situation for employees and companies."

24. When push comes to shove

Meaning: when a situation becomes serious and action is necessary.

"When push comes to shove, most people prefer job security to a high salary."

25. A hidden gem

Meaning: a surprisingly good place or thing that few people know about.

"There's a small café near my house that is a real hidden gem."

How to Use Collocations and Idioms Naturally

Memorising vocabulary is only the first step. During the IELTS Speaking test, you need to use it naturally while answering questions.

Step 1: Understand the context

Don't memorise an expression without understanding when it is appropriate. Ask yourself: "Would I actually say this in this situation?"

Step 2: Learn vocabulary in chunks

Instead of learning isolated words, learn groups of words. For example, don't simply memorise "prospects". Learn "career prospects".

Step 3: Build your own examples

After learning an expression, create a sentence connected to your own life. Personal examples are easier to remember and usually sound more natural.

Step 4: Don't overuse idioms

Important IELTS tip: Using an idiom does not automatically give you a higher band score. An unnatural or incorrectly used idiom can actually make your answer sound memorised.

Compare These Two Answers

Basic Answer More Natural Band 7+ Style
I like travelling because I can see new places. I enjoy travelling because it allows me to broaden my horizons and gain a better understanding of different cultures.
My job is good and I earn enough money. I find my job quite rewarding because it offers good career prospects and allows me to maintain a reasonable work-life balance.
Technology has good and bad sides. I'd say technology is a double-edged sword because it has made communication easier while also creating new distractions.

  • Travel: broaden my horizons, travel destination, tourist attraction

  • Society: quality of life, raise awareness, play a crucial role
  • Money: cost of living, living expenses, financial pressure

ESF Band 7+ Vocabulary Tip

Don't try to memorise all 75 expressions at once. Choose 5–10 expressions that you can genuinely use, practise them in different contexts, and gradually expand your vocabulary.

Final Thoughts

Strong IELTS Speaking vocabulary is not about using the most complicated words. It is about choosing the right words and combining them naturally.

Collocations can help you sound more natural and precise, while carefully selected idiomatic expressions can make your answers more engaging and expressive.

Most importantly, practise using these expressions in complete answers rather than simply memorising lists.

With consistent practice, you can gradually develop the flexibility and confidence needed to communicate at a higher level in the IELTS Speaking test.
